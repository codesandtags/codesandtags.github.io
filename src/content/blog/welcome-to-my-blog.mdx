After a long break from writing, I am relaunching this blog — now integrated directly into my portfolio under one unified Next.js application. No more context-switching between two separate repos, two deployment pipelines, and two design systems that slowly drifted apart.

## Why I Stopped Writing

Ironically, the reason I stopped writing was because I was learning too much, too fast. Between shipping Micro Frontend architecture across seven applications at Scotiabank, leading frontend platform work at HelloFresh, and diving deep into Backstage on Anthos — there was never a quiet moment to sit down and synthesise what I was learning into something shareable.

I kept a folder of half-finished drafts. Topics like:

- Why the strangler fig pattern is underrated for large-scale migrations
- How to interview for senior engineering roles when you are already one
- Lessons from running a frontend guild at scale

They never made it past the outline stage.

## What Changes This Time

The architecture. Literally.

By integrating the blog into `codesandtags.github.io` as a proper route (`/blog/[slug]`) rather than a separate Astro deployment, writing and publishing become part of the same `git push` workflow as updating my portfolio. No additional CI pipeline. No separate deployment to watch. Less friction means more posts.

The technical stack driving this blog:

```typescript title="src/lib/markdown/index.ts"
// Fast frontmatter extraction — no full MDX compilation for listings

  const files = (await readdir(BLOG_DIR)).filter((f) => /\.mdx?$/.test(f))
  // ...

```

Code blocks in every post ship with **zero client-side JavaScript** — syntax highlighting is done at build time by [Shiki](https://shiki.style/) via `rehype-pretty-code`. What you see is pure, pre-rendered HTML and CSS.

## What You Can Expect

Posts will cluster around three themes:

### 1. Software Architecture
The decisions I have made (and a few I regret). Topics like Micro Frontend trade-offs, how to write useful ADRs, when NOT to use event-driven architecture, and what "modular monolith" actually means in practice.

### 2. Engineering Leadership
How to grow from senior engineer to staff/principal without losing your technical depth. On running frontend guilds, doing effective architecture reviews, and the difference between influence and authority.

### 3. Ultralearning
I read Scott Young's *Ultralearning* in 2019 and it changed how I approach skill acquisition. I have applied its principles to everything from learning Angular from scratch in 90 days to picking up infrastructure tooling while shipping production features. Practical notes on deliberate practice for engineers.

## One Closing Thought

> The best way to understand something deeply is to teach it.

I have learned more from writing my [Software Architecture book notes](/blog) than from any conference talk or course. Writing forces you to find the gaps in your understanding.

More posts coming. No fixed schedule — only when there is something worth saying.
